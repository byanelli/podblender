The county almanac went to print this spring without the one thing an almanac is supposed to guarantee, a date, and nobody at the small press noticed until the first bundles were already stacked on the loading dock ready for delivery.

Readers who have relied on the slim green volume for planting schedules and tide tables took the omission in stride, with several writing to say that the weather had never much cared what the calendar claimed anyway.

The editors promised a corrected printing, though they conceded, with the dry humor the almanac is known for, that by the time it arrived the season it described would very likely have come and gone.
