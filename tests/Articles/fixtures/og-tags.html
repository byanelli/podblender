A Quiet Town Wakes To Snow

The first heavy snow of the season arrived overnight without much warning, dropping nearly a foot across the higher streets and leaving the plow crews scrambling to clear the main road before the morning commute began in earnest.

Shopkeepers along the avenue spent the early hours shoveling their storefronts and salting the icy patches that had formed in the gutters, pausing now and then to greet neighbors bundled against the unusual cold.

Schools opened two hours late, a small mercy that sent children sledding down the gentle slope behind the library while their parents traded stories about winters that were, they insisted, far worse than this one.

By midday the sun had broken through, and the town settled into the particular hush that follows a good snow, the kind that muffles the traffic and makes even the busiest corner feel like a photograph.
