The harbor master reported calm seas for the third straight day, a welcome stretch of good weather that allowed the fishing fleet to venture farther out than it had managed all month without risking the sudden squalls of early autumn.

Crews returned to the docks in the late afternoon with holds full enough to keep the processing sheds busy well past sunset, and the smell of diesel and brine hung over the waterfront as forklifts shuttled crates toward the waiting trucks.

Prices at the morning auction had held steady, buyers said, which was reason enough for the weathered captains to linger over coffee and swap the exaggerated tallies that are the closest thing the harbor has to a daily newspaper.

Forecasters warned that the calm would not last, and by the weekend a front was expected to push in from the west, but for now the fleet made the most of water as flat and bright as a sheet of hammered tin.
