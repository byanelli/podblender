The annual harvest festival drew its largest crowd in years this weekend, with families streaming into the fairgrounds from before the gates opened until well after the last of the evening light had faded behind the western hills.

Vendors reported brisk sales of everything from apple cider to hand-knit scarves, and the organizers said early ticket counts suggested attendance had nearly doubled compared with the rain-soaked event of the previous season.

Children lined up for the hayrides and the corn maze while their parents drifted between the craft stalls, and a small stage near the entrance kept up a steady rotation of local musicians throughout the long afternoon.

By the time the fireworks closed the evening, the volunteers who had spent months planning the festival allowed themselves a rare moment of rest, watching the crowd file out into the cool night satisfied and already talking about next year.
