Work on the tunnel began before anyone living in the old quarter was born, and for most of a century it went on in a way residents described as constant but invisible. The digging happened at night, and by morning the only evidence was a fine grey dust on the windowsills facing the square.

Engineers who inherited the project in its fourth decade found the original plans had been drawn for a river that had since been diverted. They spent two years surveying what was actually beneath the streets before agreeing that the route could be salvaged, though not on the alignment anyone had inherited.

The city has not published a completion date. Officials say the remaining work depends on funding that has been promised in principle and withheld in practice for six consecutive budget cycles, a pattern residents have learned to read as a schedule of its own.
